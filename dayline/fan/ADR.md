## Context

Operator asked for a dayline UX catch-up: *Sectograph, Breakdown Timer, the printout
radial bullet-journal thing — range cards, folding fans, radial-to-linear transformations.*
The dayline family already owns the problem (atlas-dayline = canonical 24h dial,
dayline = linear cockpit; see `AXIS/work/dayline/research/WAVE2-RADIALS-2026-10-06.md`).
What was missing was the **transform between the two views** — the fold itself.

## Purpose and properties

- **Purpose:** make "what did the day actually do, and how much is left" a two-second
  glance, and make the radial⇄linear transform *visible* — the fan opens and closes.
- **Properties:** 24 hour-ribs (angle = time, position preserved across the morph) ·
  events ride their ribs · now-hand pivots at the hub (dial) · dashed now-thread (card) ·
  hub readout = clock + hours left + count of things on record · one persistent control ·
  underline law respected · reduced-motion honored · no tracking, no storage, no network
  beyond its own `events.json`.
- **Data contract:** `events.json` = [{h, m, t}], max 26, generated by
  `regen-events.mjs` from `git log` (machine noise filtered). Regenerate per day:
  `node dayline/fan/regen-events.mjs`.

## Where it stopped (honest edges)

- The fold is a state jump, not the scrubbable morph the research recommends
  (`RADIAL-UX-ATOMS-2026-10-06.json` has the mechanics + code sketches, ready).
- The record is **half-blind**: git sees commits, not chat, thinking, or care.
  The instrument undercounts a life. Widening the feed (checkins, calls, daywrap)
  is the next real job, not polish.
- Verified on desktop browser only. Real-hand mobile (swipe vs tap arbitration, 9px
  hit targets) untested.
- Card mode's late-day pile-up can read as a bug; it is data. The card now says so.

## Dreams (where it wants to go)

The drag-scrub unfold (fan peels open blade-by-blade on springs), the spiral for
multi-day spans, and the what-if ring (rotate the plan, read the chain) are all
specified in the research file with sources. The version worth wearing is one where
the day is not just visible but *steerable* — move one rib, feel the rest follow.
