亗 (radical 7, 二+3, 5 strokes, cangjie input 山一一 (UMM), four-corner 22101, composition ⿱山二)
(This character, 亗, is an ancient form of 歲.)
Classical Chinese had three main demonstrative pronouns: proximal 此 (this), distal 彼 (that), and distance-neutral 是 (this or that).[6]
The frequent use of 是 as a resumptive demonstrative pronoun that reasserted the subject before a noun predicate caused it to develop into its colloquial use as a copula by the Han period and subsequently its standard use as a copula in Modern Standard Chinese.[6] 
Modern Mandarin has two main demonstratives, proximal 這/这 and distal 那; its use of the three Classical demonstratives has become mostly idiomatic,[7] although 此 continues to be used with some frequency in modern written Chinese. Cantonese uses proximal 呢 and distal 嗰 instead of 這 and 那, respectively.

Hypokeimenon (Greek: ὑποκείμενον), later often material substratum, is a term in metaphysics which literally means the "underlying thing" (Latin: subiectum).
To search for the hypokeimenon is to search for that substance that persists in a thing going through change— its basic essence.

According to Aristotle's definition,[1] hypokeimenon is something which can be predicated by other things, but cannot be a predicate of others.
The existence of a material substratum was posited by John Locke, with conceptual similarities to Baruch Spinoza's substance and Immanuel Kant's concept of the noumenon (in The Critique of Pure Reason).
Locke theorised that when all sensible properties were abstracted away from an object, such as its colour, weight, density or taste, there would still be something left to which the properties had adhered—something which allowed the object to exist independently of the sensible properties that it manifested in the beholder. Locke saw this ontological ingredient as necessary if one is to be able to consider objects as existing independently of one's own mind. The material substratum proved a difficult idea for Locke as by its very nature its existence could not be directly proven in the manner endorsed by empiricists (i.e., proof by exhibition in experience).[2] Nevertheless, he believed that the philosophical reasons for it were strong enough for its existence to be considered proved.
The existence of the substratum was denied by Berkeley. In his Three Dialogues Between Hylas and Philonous, Berkeley maintained that an object consists of nothing more than those sensible properties (or possible sensible properties) that the object manifests, and that those sensible properties only exist so long as the act of perceiving them does.

Grammatical features 
The grammatical characteristics of classical Chinese are mainly manifested in two aspects: part of speech and word order . Generally speaking, classical Chinese has more flexible use of parts of speech than vernacular .

# Nouns are used as verbs :
"The donkey can't overcome the anger, the hoof" ( Liu Zongyuan's " Three Commandments ·The Ass of Guizhou"), the noun "hooves" is used here as the verb "kick with the hoof".
"Zongjiangdong elders pity and king me" ( Sima Qian's " Historical Records · Xiang Yu Benji"), where the noun "king" is used as the causative verb "to make... king".
"Seeing that his hair hits eighty-nine out of ten, but he has a slight shackle" ( Ouyang Xiu's " Selling Oil Weng "). "Nod" was originally the noun "chin", and the verb "nod" is used here.
# Nouns are used as adverbs , often used with "ran" (the appearance of...):
"When I was young, a wolf walked away, and the other dog was sitting in front." ( Pu Songling, " Strange Tales from a Lonely Studio "), the noun "dog" is used as an adverb "like a dog" before the verb "sit".
"Things turn round and round, there is a pavilion whose wings are on the spring, and the Zui Weng Pavilion is also." ( Ouyang Xiu "The Story of Zui Weng Pavilion "), "wing" is used as the adverb "like wings (wings)" before the verb "lin".
# The object is prefaced. Purpose: to make sentences smooth. To emphasize the object , advance it. The auxiliary word is usually added .
Xun Yan said: "The cock crows and drives, and the base is at the stove. The only horse head is looking forward." ("Zuo Zhuan·Xianggong Fourteen Years"), "the horse head is looking forward" means "seeing the horse head". "Yes" is the auxiliary word.
# In interrogative sentences, interrogative pronouns such as "who", "孰", "he", "xi", etc. are placed before the verb:
"The minister is not talented, but who dares to complain?" (" Zuo Zhuan ")
"Who am I deceiving? Deceiving the sky!" (The Analects )
"Weiss people, who am I going to return?" ( Fan Zhongyan " Yueyang Tower ")
"I am what with North Xu Mei?" ( " Warring · Zou Ji satirical King of Qi remonstrance")
"Wang said:'Bound those who are bound by "what" is the one?" (" Yanzi Chunqiu ")
# In negative sentences using pronouns as the object , the generally placed verb front
A gentleman is sick and incompetent, even if he doesn’t know the patient ("The Analects ")
# Inverted sentence used with "以":
"The whole stone is the bottom, near the shore, and the bottom of the scroll is out." (Liu Zongyuan "The Eight Records of Yongzhou ·Xiaoshitan") "That is, "roll out of the stone bottom."
# Sometimes attributive rear of usage, post-attributive often preceded by "the" or later will add "by" [5] :
If the plan is undecided, if you ask someone to report to Qin, you have not got it. ( Sima Qian " Historical ‧ Lian Po Lin Hsiang-ju")

表现形式
 
一）义符和声之规
左形右声，如钱、材、消、熔等；
右形左声，如放、鸭、刎、颈等；
上形下声，如茅、简、空、室等；
下形上声，如柴、酱、盆、甕等；
内形外声，如闻、问、闽等；
外形内声，如辟匪、匐、固等。
有的形符与声符处在一个角落，
如颖，从禾顷声 脩，从肉攸声； 赖，从贝刺声；听，从听壬声。
有的形符被声符隔离，
如衷，从衣中声 街，从行圭声 哀，从口衣声 衡，从 行声。对这样的形声字在分析形体时一定要注意。
二）形符或声符字的形体
为了养活某个形声字的笔划或构形的美观，在汉字形体演变的某一阶段上，形符或声符被部分地省简而成为所谓省形或省声。如弑，弑省，式声；耆，从老省，旨声；产，从生，彦省声；夜，从夕亦省声，等等。
省形和省声，在汉字的发展、使用过程中确实存在，了解这种情况对研究字形与字义的关系颇为有益。但是，省是以不省为前提的，在没有发现不省的字形以前，不可轻言省形或省声，否则容易犯主观臆测的错误。许慎在《说文》中对省形省声就用得过滥，有的颇令人怀疑（如说頫字是“从逃省”，妃字是“配省声”）。有的则是明显的错误（如说弗字）是“从 省”，监字是“ 省声”，奔字是“贲省声”等）。所以对《说文》之言省，特别是省声，我们不可轻信。
三） 形符或声符形态
如恭是从心共声，唐是从口庚声，徒是从辵土声，归是从帚追声，责是从贝声，等等。有的形符或声符则完全被省简隐没，根本认不出是形声字了，如书本为从聿、者声，泰本为从水、大声。对于这样的表声字，必须复形到变之前的形体，不好辨识。
