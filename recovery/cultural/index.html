The Bao & Noodle saga 包子與麵條

46 parts. 41 recovered from six sources. Missing: 19, 25, 26, 27, 28.

No attributable author. The Chinese collector says so himself — 原作者不知誰. Earliest Singapore text is a 2003 forum thread.

The mechanism. Not one joke. A serial war of mistaken identity in which every character is beaten for a visual feature they cannot help, because the attacker believes it is a disguise. The enoki is beaten for wearing a hat. The meatball is beaten for having no wrapper. The bread is beaten by both sides, because 面 is noodle and 包 is bun, and he is both at once.

The war ends in a romance between winter noodles and a rice cake. Their child is a sea-bream plate.

Rasa & Malay poetics rasa · hikayat · pantun

Rasa is the Sanskrit/Malay poetics of aesthetic appreciation — the flavour a work evokes in its audience. Here it is a mechanism, not a mood tag.

The Rasa Sequencer. Instead of a static MOOD = awe, a trajectory: BASELINE → expectancy → perturbation → withholding → bodily cue → recognition → intensification → release / nonrelease, over rhythm, density, interval, gesture, image, voice, proximity, information, repetition, silence.

The thread also produced a Malay hikayat-form narrative, Kembangan di Mulut Naga (The Bloom in the Dragon's Mouth), and a pantun/anthem, Kembangan Berapi.

Provenance discipline

This material is RECOVER-DONOR. It is preserved, not promoted. Recovering something does not make it canonical, and the Singapore and Chinese traditions are related but not identical — they are kept separate rather than merged into one lineage. Constructed works (the 512 anthology, the Jinshi foundry, the Rasa Sequencer) are labelled as the operator's own and are never presented as recovered external canon.
