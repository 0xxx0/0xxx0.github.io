# 1. CRITIQUE + STRENGTHEN

## 1.1 Proposition audit

### K001 — perceptual hierarchy

**Seed:** position → position nonaligned → length → angle/slope → area → volume/color saturation. fileciteturn0file0L3-L4

**Verdict: P/V, but “strict hierarchy” should be deleted.**

Classic graphical-perception experiments strongly support position and length as excellent encodings for quantitative comparison; position judgments were especially accurate. citeturn823116search4turn823116search2

But newer work shows meaningful **individual differences**: some observers' performance departs from the canonical ordering. A ranking therefore describes a population-level prior, not a universal human law. citeturn155943search0turn155943search1

**Replace with:**

> For precise quantitative comparison, prefer common-scale position and length as strong defaults. Treat graphical-channel rankings as task-, viewer-, and context-conditioned priors rather than immutable laws.

**Confidence: High.**

S=The hierarchy survives, but changes from commandment to prior.

---

### K002 — salience ≠ relevance

**Seed:** visually prominent but irrelevant information produces judgment errors. fileciteturn0file0L5-L5

**Verdict: P; causal magnitude underspecified.**

The principle is structurally sound: an interface fails whenever attention allocation and decision relevance diverge. But the seed wording implies a quantified general effect without specifying task, viewer, or effect size.

Very recent work is moving toward explicit mechanistic models of this problem. A July 2026 preprint models chart reading as visual search and produces a simulated **tick-salience bias**, although that work is new and should not yet be treated as settled evidence. citeturn264976academia76

**Upgrade:**

> Design for *salience–relevance alignment*, then validate whether irrelevant elements capture attention or alter decisions.

**Confidence: High as design principle; Medium for general quantitative claims.**

---

### K003 — enclosure dominates grouping

**Seed:** enclosure/common region is “the most powerful” grouping mechanism. fileciteturn0file0L6-L6

**Verdict: V/P; wording too absolute.**

Palmer showed common region can overcome proximity and similarity. citeturn823116search0 Later experiments found common region dominating connectedness in a particular competition paradigm. citeturn823116search12

That supports **strong extrinsic grouping cue**, not “universally strongest Gestalt principle.”

**Replace with:**

> Common region is a particularly strong grouping operation and should be preferred when unambiguous grouping is essential.

---

### K004 — conjunctive search

**Seed:** finding a target defined by several features requires more concentration and memory. fileciteturn0file0L7-L7

**Verdict: V, but rewrite mechanism.**

Feature-integration research supports the stronger and more precise formulation: conjunctions of multiple features generally require focused/selective attention rather than the relatively efficient detection available for certain single-feature targets. citeturn264976search0turn264976search3

**Design consequence:** avoid making critical discrimination depend on *finding red + triangular + striped* among visually similar glyphs.

---

### K005 — whole before parts

**Seed:** global structures are identified before local features. fileciteturn0file0L8-L8

**Verdict: P/V, not universal.**

Global-precedence effects are well established, but their strength varies with stimulus and observer conditions. citeturn216698search0

**Upgrade:**

> Assume the initial glance extracts coarse configuration before detailed semantics; verify rather than assuming universal global precedence.

This is extremely useful for poster, plate, dashboard and glyph composition.

---

### K006 — dictionary compression → visual macros

**Seed:** recurring motifs can be replaced with macros. fileciteturn0file0L9-L9

**Verdict: move from K → S/P.**

The underlying family of techniques is real. Dictionary-compression research has been used to expose recurrent patterns in logs, and recent graph systems replace repeated subgraphs with adaptive motifs to simplify large graphs. citeturn801641academia51turn801641search0turn801641search6

But:

> “Compression reduces cognitive load”

is not automatically true.

A bad macro merely replaces visible complexity with **decoding complexity**.

**New rule:** compression is valid only if retrieval cost + ambiguity cost < complexity removed.

S=Visual compression becomes a semantic compiler, not a shrinking operation.

---

### K007 — typography

**Seed:** typography behaves like speaking style; 1 typeface and 2–3 sizes unify complex posters. fileciteturn0file0L10-L10

**Verdict: M/X, not K.**

The design intuition is useful; the numerical restriction is a style heuristic rather than a strong perceptual law.

**Replace with a role system:**

- display
- section
- body
- annotation
- metadata/code

The important invariant is **consistent semantic role → consistent typographic treatment**, not an arbitrary number of font sizes.

### K008 / F004 — SparkWords

The distinction between **legibility** and **readability** is useful. The stronger claim that size variation inherently destroys readability should remain restricted to inline/prose-like SparkWord contexts, not typography generally. fileciteturn0file0L11-L11 fileciteturn0file0L29-L29

## 1.2 Failure audit

### F001 — “avoid 3D”

The source says 3D tends to muddle high-dimensional biological data. fileciteturn0file0L25-L26

Rewrite:

> Avoid **unnecessary perspective/depth encoding for abstract quantitative comparisons**.

Do **not** prohibit 3D when physical 3D structure is itself the phenomenon: anatomy, molecular geometry, spatial trajectories, architecture, field topology.

**Rule:** simulated space must encode spatial meaning, not spectacle.

---

### F002 — semantic satiation

The proposed transfer from repeated words to repeated symbols is presently an **analogy**, not an established visualization law. fileciteturn0file0L27-L27

Rename it:

> **Symbol-field collapse:** excessive repetition can reduce individuation and turn meaningful marks into texture.

Test it experimentally rather than citing semantic satiation as the mechanism.

---

### F003 — chartjunk

The seed makes an absolute anti-decoration claim. fileciteturn0file0L28-L28

That is too strong.

Bateman et al. found embellished charts were not less accurate in their experimental task and showed better long-term recall than plain versions. citeturn216698search1turn216698search3

Therefore:

> Decoration is not intrinsically bad. **Interference is bad.**

Aesthetic complexity is permissible when it:
1. preserves quantitative geometry,
2. does not mask distinctions,
3. does not compete with task-critical salience,
4. preferably reinforces structure or memorability.

This is important for experimental visual systems: rigor does **not** require sterility.

# 2. DESIGN SYSTEM v1.1

## 2.1 Compilation pipeline

Every artifact passes through:

**DATA → TASK → SEMANTIC TYPES → CHANNEL ALLOCATION → GROUPING → COMPRESSION → SALIENCE → AESTHETIC SHELL → VALIDATION**

Never begin from “what looks interesting?”

Begin from:

> **What distinction must survive?**

---

## 2.2 Semantic types

Each visual datum receives one primary type:

| Type | Question |
|---|---|
| QNT | how much? |
| ORD | in what order? |
| CAT | which kind? |
| ID | which individual? |
| REL | connected to what? |
| GEO | where? |
| TMP | when / sequence? |
| UNC | how uncertain? |
| CAU | what acts on what? |
| STA | what state? |
| EPI | how do we know? |

This prevents the S001 failure where “channel strength” is applied without considering what the channel must communicate.

The seed already argues for hierarchy-to-hierarchy mapping. fileciteturn0file0L13-L16 The upgrade is **type-conditioned mapping**.

---

## 2.3 Channel allocator

### Quantitative
Prefer:

**position → length → carefully controlled angle/area → luminance**

Do not encode precision primarily through arbitrary hue.

### Ordinal
Prefer:

position / sequence / length / monotonic luminance.

### Categorical
Prefer:

spatial region / hue / shape / texture.

### Relational
Prefer:

connection / adjacency / enclosure / directed path.

### Critical state
Use **redundant encoding**.

WCAG 2.2 explicitly requires that color not be the sole visual means of conveying information. citeturn184169search0turn184169search1

New 2026 CHI evidence also finds carefully paired color+shape redundancy can improve categorical judgments, particularly in some medium-category-count conditions; pairings themselves matter. citeturn184169search2turn184169academia35

---

## 2.4 Salience budget

Define every element's:

- decision relevance **R**
- desired salience **S**
- actual observed salience **Ŝ**

A design fails where:

**Ŝ ≫ R** — distraction  
or  
**Ŝ ≪ R** — invisibility.

### Proposed metric — Salience Misalignment

\[
SM=\sum_i w_i |\hat S_i-R_i|
\]

**N — new construct, not an established perceptual metric.**

The seed Squint Test is therefore retained as a fast proxy for estimating Ŝ. fileciteturn0file0L18-L20

---

## 2.5 Grouping grammar

Use grouping intentionally:

**strong boundary required**
→ common region / enclosure

**soft association**
→ proximity / alignment / whitespace

**process**
→ connection / direction

**hierarchy**
→ nested region + scale + alignment

**exception**
→ break established grouping rule deliberately

Do not simultaneously establish contradictory groups with shape, enclosure, proximity and connection.

---

## 2.6 Whitespace

The seed correctly promotes whitespace from “unused area” to active structure. fileciteturn0file0L21-L21

Upgrade:

Whitespace may encode:

- separation
- hierarchy
- pause
- exceptional status
- reading order
- uncertainty between clusters

But remove the unsupported universal claim that whitespace is *always* stronger than brightness.

---

## 2.7 Typography grammar

Typography becomes semantic infrastructure:

**T0 — identity/title**  
**T1 — navigation/section**  
**T2 — claim/data label**  
**T3 — explanatory prose**  
**T4 — provenance/meta**

Rule:

> Equal semantic roles receive equal typography.

Variation without semantic reason is **typographic entropy**.

---

## 2.8 Compression grammar

The source distinguishes compaction from summarization. fileciteturn0file0L57-L59

Add a third form:

### 1. Compaction
same information, reduced physical space.

### 2. Summarization
information intentionally deleted.

### 3. Macroization
recurrent structure replaced by a reusable semantic token.

Macroization is the most interesting mode.

A macro may compile only when:

- structure recurs,
- semantic types match,
- exceptions remain visible,
- expansion is possible when necessary,
- the symbol costs less to decode than the structure it replaces.

Recent motif-based graph simplification provides a concrete computational analogue. citeturn801641search6

S=Compression succeeds when it lowers *meaning-recovery cost*, not mark count.

# 3. APPLICATION — GLYPHS / STENCILS / PRISON AGE VISUAL SYSTEM

## 3.1 Three-shell glyph architecture

Every glyph receives three concentric informational layers:

### CORE
Survives extreme reduction.

Carries only:
- identity/class
- major state
- critical polarity

### STRUCTURE
Appears at medium resolution.

Carries:
- subtype
- direction
- relation
- magnitude

### ATMOSPHERE
Appears at large resolution.

Carries:
- texture
- narrative
- ornament
- secondary metadata
- moiré/fractal/detail structures

This solves a recurring conflict between **high aesthetic complexity** and **small-scale recognizability**.

Never place inference-critical information exclusively in ATMOSPHERE.

---

## 3.2 Replace the single Crush Test

The seed proposes testing at 10×10 or 5×5 px. fileciteturn0file0L19-L20

Keep it—but reinterpret the thresholds as internal stress tests, not biological constants.

### Resolution Invariance Profile

Test each glyph at:

**5 → 10 → 20 → 40 → 80 px**

Record which semantic fields survive.

Example:

| Scale | Must survive |
|---:|---|
| 5 px | class silhouette |
| 10 px | class + polarity |
| 20 px | state |
| 40 px | secondary variables |
| 80 px | full narrative/detail |

This produces a **semantic degradation curve**, which is far more informative than PASS/FAIL at 10 px.

---

## 3.3 Error-correcting glyph assignment

The seed proposes maximizing Hamming distance. fileciteturn0file0L35-L36

Upgrade substantially.

Pure Hamming distance treats every visual bit as equally noticeable and every confusion as equally costly.

Instead define:

\[
Cost(a,b)=P(confuse\ a,b)\times Harm(confuse\ a,b)
\]

Assign the most perceptually distant glyphs to the classes for which confusion would be most damaging.

This is **error-correcting visual coding**.

It converts glyph generation from:

> “make symbols different”

into:

> “spend visual distance where mistakes cost most.”

---

## 3.4 Stencil grammar

A stencil adds fabrication constraints after perception constraints:

**semantic topology**
→ **visual skeleton**
→ **bridge topology**
→ **minimum stroke**
→ **minimum gap**
→ **physical material**
→ **render scale**

Therefore a successful glyph needs two representations:

1. **semantic master**
2. **fabrication-safe derivative**

Do not deform the semantic master merely because one production medium requires bridges.

---

## 3.5 Information-rich Prison Age plates

Use five depth bands:

**B0 — gestalt:** immediate total shape  
**B1 — navigation:** where to look  
**B2 — proposition:** principal information  
**B3 — evidence:** mechanisms/data/relations  
**B4 — archaeology:** microtext, hidden mappings, narrative texture

This makes dense work **progressively penetrable** rather than merely dense.

A viewer should receive:

- 1 second → orientation
- 5 seconds → principal claim
- 20 seconds → system
- 2 minutes → evidence
- prolonged inspection → hidden structure

This follows the seed's global-first intuition while turning it into a production protocol.

---

## 3.6 Moiré / op-art / fractal elements

Permit them—but compile them as **X = expressive layers**.

They may encode:

- instability
- phase transition
- field intensity
- recursion
- uncertainty

only when those mappings remain consistent.

Otherwise they remain atmosphere.

**Hard constraint:** no high-frequency texture may intersect a precision-reading zone unless tested for interference.

This preserves the very thing strict “chartjunk” doctrine tends to destroy: memorable visual character.

# 5. 2026 RESEARCH EXPANSION

## Δ1 — canonical encoding rankings need personalization

The strongest correction to the original source comes from Davis et al.: visualization skill varies enough between individuals that canonical encoding rankings should not be treated as universally ordered. citeturn155943search0turn155943search1

### Consequence
Eventually, interfaces could perform a short **perceptual calibration** and alter their visual grammar for the actual observer.

That moves visualization from:

**universal design prior**

toward

**observer-adaptive encoding**.

---

## Δ2 — glyph design is inherently multi-objective

Hsieh, Zavatsky and Chen propose multi-criteria decision analysis for glyph design precisely because glyph quality cannot be reduced to one metric. citeturn155943academia76

So reject:

> maximize Hamming distance → solved.

Use a vector:

\[
V=[D,\ R,\ S,\ A,\ T,\ M,\ E]
\]

where:

- **D** discriminability
- **R** resolution robustness
- **S** semantic fit
- **A** accessibility
- **T** task performance
- **M** memorability
- **E** expressive/aesthetic quality

Do not silently collapse this vector to one scalar unless a specific task supplies the weights.

---

## Δ3 — motif compression is now operational, not merely metaphorical

AdaMotif automatically extracts similar graph substructures, represents clusters with adaptive motifs, and reports user-study evidence that simplification can preserve important structural/community information. citeturn801641search6

Dictionary-compression work similarly extracts recurring textual patterns for visualization. citeturn801641search0turn801641academia51

Thus the source's compression idea should be promoted from interesting analogy to an actual research/development track.

---

## Δ4 — redundant encoding deserves rehabilitation

The seed correctly recommends redundant encoding for critical information. fileciteturn0file0L64-L69

WCAG establishes the accessibility case for not relying solely on color. citeturn184169search0

The 2026 CatPAW work adds empirical support that color+shape redundancy can improve some categorical judgments—but also shows interactions between particular shapes and colors, so merely “adding another channel” is insufficient. citeturn184169search2

**Upgrade:** redundant channels must themselves be tested as a pair.

---

## Δ5 — chart minimalism is not the objective function

Bateman et al. remains an important falsifier of simplistic chartjunk doctrine: embellishment can coexist with interpretation accuracy and improve memorability under some conditions. citeturn216698search1

Therefore our objective is:

\[
\text{maximum useful cognition}
\]

not:

\[
\text{minimum ink}
\]

That distinction opens much more interesting territory.

---

## Δ6 — visual cognition can become executable

The July 2026 Active-Inference work attempts to turn visualization interpretation into inspectable computational agents balancing uncertainty reduction and cognitive effort. citeturn264976academia76

This is preliminary, but conceptually important.

Instead of asking only:

> “Did users understand the chart?”

we can increasingly ask:

> “What sequence of attention, uncertainty and memory operations predicts the error?”

That suggests a future **visualization linter with cognitive simulations**.

# 6. DEEP EXCAVATION — NEW DEDUCTIONS

Everything below is **N = proposed synthesis**, not established fact.

---

## N001 — Visual systems are error-correcting codes

A glyph vocabulary is structurally similar to a communication code:

**semantic state → visual codeword → noisy channel → observer decoding**

Noise includes:

- scale
- blur
- printing
- poor contrast
- distraction
- color-vision differences
- memory
- crowding
- unfamiliarity

Therefore glyph design should explicitly optimize **error recovery**, not beauty or distinctiveness alone.

This is the deeper form of A001.

---

## N002 — Perceptual checksum

Encode mission-critical state through two sufficiently independent cues.

Example:

**state = dangerous**
→ topology change + label

If one channel vanishes during printing or reduction, the other remains.

If they disagree, the artifact contains an internal error signal.

This is the visualization equivalent of a checksum.

---

## N003 — Semantic compression must be type-preserving

Suppose two graph motifs are structurally isomorphic:

\[
G_a \cong G_b
\]

but one represents **causal inhibition** and one represents **organizational ownership**.

They must not compile to the same macro.

Define:

\[
Macro(a)=Macro(b)
\]

only if both:

\[
Structure(a)\approx Structure(b)
\]

and

\[
SemanticSignature(a)=SemanticSignature(b)
\]

This formalizes the source's type-sensitive motif insight. fileciteturn0file0L14-L16

---

## N004 — Resolution is a semantic dimension

Traditional design treats resolution as an output constraint.

Instead:

> each scale reveals a deliberate semantic layer.

Then zooming is not merely magnification.

It becomes **semantic disclosure**.

This is ideal for:
- maps
- scientific atlases
- glyph fields
- dashboards
- visual story systems
- large-format narrative plates.

---

## N005 — Aesthetic complexity should form a shell

Instead of choosing between:

**rigorous scientific diagram**
versus
**beautiful experimental image**

build:

\[
CORE_{truth}
\subset
STRUCTURE_{reasoning}
\subset
SHELL_{expression}
\]

The shell may be extremely elaborate as long as information can be recovered by progressively removing it.

### Shell-stripping test

Remove in sequence:

1. ornament
2. textures
3. secondary color
4. atmospheric marks
5. annotations

If the principal inference survives until the semantic core remains, complexity is controlled.

---

## N006 — Confusion cost should determine visual distance

Current glyph systems often maximize average separation.

Wrong objective.

If confusing A with B is harmless but confusing A with C is catastrophic, then:

\[
D(A,C) \gg D(A,B)
\]

This creates **anisotropic glyph space**: visual distance follows semantic consequence.

This is probably the strongest practical extension of the original Hamming-distance proposal.

---

## N007 — Information density ≠ mark density

Define:

\[
UsefulDensity=
\frac{decision\text{-}relevant\ distinctions}
{perceptual\ effort}
\]

A poster containing 5,000 meaningful marks may outperform one containing 50 poorly organized marks.

Therefore “minimal” and “dense” are almost irrelevant descriptors without a task model.

---

## N008 — Salience is a finite budget

Increasing one element's salience changes the relative salience of everything else.

Therefore emphasis is not additive.

It is competitive.

Treat prominence like resource allocation:

\[
\sum_i S_i \leq B_{attention}
\]

Again: conceptual model, not established quantitative law.

This explains why uncontrolled gradients, textures, borders and saturated colors can make *everything* visible but **nothing dominant**.

---

## N009 — The legend is a decompressor

A legend is not merely explanatory text.

It reconstructs semantic meaning from compressed marks.

Therefore evaluate:

\[
CompressionBenefit
-
LegendLookupCost
\]

A visual macro that forces continual legend consultation may be a net expansion of cognitive work.

The ideal mature glyph becomes partially self-decoding through consistent morphology.

---

## N010 — Visual grammar can learn a viewer

Davis et al.'s individual-difference results imply a future adaptive system:

1. show microtasks,
2. estimate observer-specific confusion,
3. derive personal channel performance,
4. recompile glyphs/charts,
5. periodically recalibrate.

Thus there may eventually be no single “best” glyph alphabet.

There may be a **base language + observer-specific font**.
