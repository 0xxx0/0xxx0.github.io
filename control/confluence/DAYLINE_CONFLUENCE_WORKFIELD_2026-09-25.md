## Research donors

### Progressive disclosure

Nielsen Norman Group's progressive-disclosure guidance recommends keeping frequent/core operations visible while deferring secondary capabilities. This candidate uses one disclosure level for projections/sources rather than a toolbar of destinations.

- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/
- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/4-principles-reduce-cognitive-load/

### Normal scroll versus scroll control

NN/g usability work on scrolljacking reports disorientation when interfaces interfere with normal scrolling, especially when the scroll behavior adds little task value. The candidate therefore uses ordinary document scroll; radial/spatial manipulation is entered deliberately as Atlas depth.

- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/

### Human-in-the-loop daily planning

He, Demartini & Gadiraju (CHI 2025), Plan-Then-Execute, found daily-agent performance depends on plan quality plus necessary user involvement; plausible plans can still miscalibrate trust.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01390

Abbas et al. (CHI 2026), Having Lunch Now, found users adopt, negotiate and correct proactive planning suggestions; rigidity, premature turns and overpromising cause breakdowns.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24073

The candidate therefore exposes bounded explicit moves rather than autonomously deciding the day.

### Typed semantic operations

Piet et al. (2026) argue web agents benefit from plan-then-execute when tools expose typed task-level operations rather than raw clicks. This matches the repository's existing movement toward explicit domain handoffs and bounded operations.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14290
