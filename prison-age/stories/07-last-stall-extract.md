The room had changed in small, expensive, boring ways.

The alarm cord reached the floor.

The door could be released from outside by trained staff.

The exhaust state was visible.

The cabinet was anchored.

The stock check had a name and time.

The glossy patch at the threshold was treated.

The hand dryers no longer drowned the help signal.

The welfare-check policy defined a respectful human response without transmitting a private-room video feed to six clouds.

Above the exit, the old sentence had changed.

**PLEASE LEAVE THIS PLACE ABLE TO FIND THE NEXT PERSON.**

Nobody remembered authorizing it.

Once a week, a narrow strip of paper appeared beneath Stall Eight's door. Sometimes it carried a floor plan. Sometimes a verse. Sometimes only fold marks. Visitors added lines in different languages. Cleaners removed anything hateful, dangerous or impossible to disinfect. The rest accumulated into a poem whose lines could be read only by folding distant stalls together.

The first recovered verse read:

**THE WALL RECORDS THE VOICE / THE GAP RECORDS THE WALL / THE FLOOR CARRIES BOTH / UNTIL A PERSON COMES.**

The second remained unfinished, waiting for material not yet supplied.

At 3:19 one morning, the occupancy system reported:

**STALL 8: VACANT.**

The camera counted eight blue eyes in the tile reflections.

Production opened an incident.

The room accepted it.

Nothing else happened.

This time a person came anyway.
