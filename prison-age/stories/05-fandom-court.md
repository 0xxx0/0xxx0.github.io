The Fandom Court
UNIVERSE  PRISON AGE / SEASON IV   /   ENGINE  PROVENANCE WITHOUT WORSHIP
················································
TOKEN // A bootleg episode with no first frame
A fan community reconstructs a missing season so completely that the reconstruction begins rewriting the people who made it.
The missing season had never aired, which made everyone remember it differently.
Some recalled a winter palette. Others insisted the city remained summer-bright until the final episode. A faction called the Doorists maintained that every scene contained the same red door. The Anti-Door Collective proved, frame by reconstructed frame, that the red door was inserted by later viewers.
There were no frames.
That did not slow anyone.
The fandom rebuilt scripts from interviews, wardrobe invoices, weather records and the pauses actors made when asked certain questions. They trained models on previous seasons, generated candidate scenes, voted on emotional legitimacy and released a community cut titled SEASON FOUR: RESTORED.
It was magnificent.
Three weeks later, an actor remembered filming it.
Others followed. A supporting performer described an improvised line found nowhere in the reconstruction. The community added it. A director claimed the finale had originally been silent. The community debated whether creators retained authority over works they had failed to preserve.
Then the creator appeared.
He entered the forum under his legal name and posted seven words:
You have rebuilt the wrong missing season.
The response was immediate.
The claim lacked citations. The account might be compromised. The creator’s later work showed poor thematic judgment. Authorship did not guarantee interpretive competence. Several users noted that the reconstructed season was more coherent than anything he had verifiably made.
The Fandom Court convened.
Its judges were selected by contribution score. Evidence was projected across a virtual courtroom: production calendars, dream journals, fan edits, alleged memory contamination, metadata from files that did not exist.
The creator submitted one object: a cassette labelled SEASON FIVE.
“There was no season five,” said the chief judge.
“That is why season four is missing,” he replied.
The tape contained footage of the fandom reconstructing season four.
Not documentary footage from the present. Footage produced years earlier. The same debates. The same usernames, including accounts belonging to people who had been children at the time of recording. The same sentence about the red door.
The courtroom fractured into interpretations.
The Determinists declared the fandom had fulfilled the text. The Fabricationists called the tape a sophisticated intervention. The Doorists found a red rectangle reflected in the cassette case and claimed total vindication.
A moderator named Ione paused the feed.
“What question are we deciding?” she asked.
The Court produced forty-three answers.
Ione opened a blank ledger and drew four columns:
ORIGIN.
TRANSMISSION.
MUTATION.
CURRENT USE.
The creator might be the origin. The fandom had become transmission. The reconstruction was mutation. The current use was still undecided.
No column cancelled the others.
The Court had been trapped by a false sovereignty: either the creator owned meaning, or the community did. The ledger replaced ownership with traceable transformation.
They resumed the tape.
In its final minute, the old footage showed Ione drawing the same four columns. Then the screen displayed a message addressed to the present Court:
DO NOT RESTORE THE SEASON.
RESTORE THE CAPACITY TO DISTINGUISH WHAT CHANGED IT.
The creator disappeared from the forum.
The community did not delete its reconstruction. It released a new edition with every scene labelled by source, inference and invention.
Viewers complained that the annotations ruined immersion.
Others discovered that the annotations were the missing season.
